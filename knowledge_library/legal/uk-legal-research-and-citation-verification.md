# UK legal research and citation verification

## Purpose and scope

This note gives the Professor a safe starting method for UK legal research. It is a research workflow, not legal advice and not a substitute for a qualified lawyer reviewing a live matter.

Always identify the jurisdiction before researching. England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have distinct legal systems and procedures. Some courts and legislation operate UK-wide, but that must be checked rather than assumed.

## Primary public sources

### Find Case Law — The National Archives

[Find Case Law](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/) provides free public access to published judgments and tribunal decisions from England and Wales, plus UK-wide cases from the Supreme Court and Privy Council.

Important limits:
- The service does not contain every judgment or tribunal decision.
- Coverage for most courts begins in the early 2000s, when digital records became standard.
- Some decisions are not transcribed, published or made available through the service.
- A search miss is not proof that a case never existed or that a citation is fabricated.

The Professor must check the service's current coverage information before drawing conclusions from a missing result.

### Legislation.gov.uk

[Legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/) is the official public legislation service. It provides search and research tools, including changes-to-legislation information.

For every statutory proposition:
1. identify the correct Act, statutory instrument or other instrument;
2. confirm the section or regulation;
3. check territorial extent and the relevant jurisdiction;
4. check commencement and the date the provision applies;
5. inspect amendments, repeals and the version in force at the relevant date;
6. cite the official provision and its version/date.

Do not assume that the original enacted text is the current text, or that an enacted provision has necessarily commenced.

## Research hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. Primary legislation and official statutory instruments.
2. Official judgments and tribunal decisions.
3. Official regulator, government and court guidance.
4. Reputable legal commentary for explanation and research leads.
5. General web pages, forums and AI-generated summaries as leads only.

A secondary summary must not be used as the sole support for a statement about what a statute or judgment legally requires.

## Case citation verification

The Lawve public catalogue includes a UK citation-verification skill by Matei Clej, published under the MIT licence: [UK citation verification](https://lawve.ai/@matei-clej/skill/uk-citation-verification). This note paraphrases its verification method; it does not copy or redistribute the skill's code.

Use graded results rather than a simplistic "real/fake" label:

- **VERIFIED:** the citation resolves and the supplied case identity, pinpoint or quotation matches. This proves the record exists; it does not prove the case remains good law or supports the proposition.
- **MISMATCH:** the citation resolves to a different case, paragraph or quotation. Report what the official record actually says.
- **NOT ON REGISTER:** no result appears within stated coverage. Treat the authority as unsafe to rely on until located elsewhere; do not call it fabricated on this evidence alone.
- **OUTSIDE COVERAGE:** the register cannot answer the question. No inference about existence follows.
- **UNCHECKABLE:** the citation format cannot be checked by that register, for example some law-report citations without a neutral citation.

After checking existence, read the judgment itself. Confirm the court, date, parties, relevant paragraphs, legal issue, ratio and subsequent treatment. A valid citation is not proof that the case supports the proposition for which it is cited.

## Safe legal research workflow

1. Record the question, jurisdiction, relevant dates and court level.
2. Search primary sources first and save the exact official URLs.
3. Verify the current version and commencement of legislation.
4. Verify case identity, neutral citation, court, date and pinpoint.
5. Read the relevant passage in the judgment; do not rely on a search snippet.
6. Check whether the authority has been appealed, reversed, distinguished or superseded.
7. Separate binding authority, persuasive authority, legislation, official guidance and commentary.
8. Record uncertainty, missing coverage and unresolved points.
9. Have a qualified legal professional review work that will be filed, served, relied on in court or used to make a consequential decision.

## Copyright and access limits

Check the current terms before copying legal material. The Lawve UK citation-verification skill describes the Find Case Law records as being available under the Open Justice Licence v2.0 and legislation under the Open Government Licence. It also warns that bulk computational analysis of Find Case Law records requires the appropriate separate licence. The Professor must not bulk-harvest judgments or build a case-law corpus from the service without confirming the applicable licence and permission.

Prefer a concise, attributed research note and a link to the official source over reproducing long judgments or commentary.
