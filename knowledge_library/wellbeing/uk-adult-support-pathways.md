# UK adult mental-health information and support pathways

**Document status:** curated reference note for the initial Wellbeing Knowledge Division. This is public-service signposting, not a clinical assessment or treatment recommendation. England-specific pathways are labelled. Recheck all routes before release.

## Scope

This note records a small set of official NHS pathways that the Professor may explain in general terms. It does not determine whether a particular service is clinically suitable for an individual. Local availability and eligibility can vary.

## NHS Talking Therapies in England

The NHS explains that talking therapies are delivered by trained professionals and can support people with a range of difficulties, including anxiety and depression. In England, adults may be able to refer themselves to NHS Talking Therapies for anxiety and depression; access criteria and routes can vary, and the service page explains local pathways.

Official information:
- NHS Talking therapies: https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/talking-therapies/
- Find NHS Talking Therapies for anxiety and depression: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/mental-health-services/find-nhs-talking-therapies-for-anxiety-and-depression/
- NHS England service overview: https://www.england.nhs.uk/mental-health/adults/nhs-talking-therapies/

The Professor may help a user locate the official information or prepare questions. It must not promise acceptance, diagnose eligibility, or claim a referral has been made.

## Urgent mental-health help in England

The NHS states that a person needing urgent mental-health help can use NHS 111 online or call 111 and select the mental-health option. The service can direct them to the appropriate help and may connect them with a trained mental-health professional.

Official information:
- NHS urgent help: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/mental-health-services/where-to-get-urgent-help-for-mental-health/

## Immediate danger or life at risk

The NHS advises calling 999 or going to A&E if someone's life is at risk, including where they have seriously injured themselves or taken an overdose, or where they do not feel able to keep themselves or someone else safe. A mental-health emergency should be treated as seriously as a physical-health emergency.

The Professor must not delay this direction with lengthy questions, a questionnaire or an AI risk score. It must not imply that it has contacted emergency services unless a separately verified integration has actually done so.

Official information:
- NHS urgent help: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/mental-health-services/where-to-get-urgent-help-for-mental-health/

## Listening support

The NHS lists Samaritans on 116 123 as a free listening service. This is a listening/support option, not an emergency response service and not a replacement for 999, A&E, NHS 111 or a person's care team.

Official information:
- NHS urgent help: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/mental-health-services/where-to-get-urgent-help-for-mental-health/

## Operational safeguards

1. Confirm the user's country/region before giving location-specific pathways, or label the default jurisdiction explicitly.
2. Keep emergency, urgent and non-urgent pathways distinct.
3. Show the source link and the last-verified date.
4. Recheck contact routes at least monthly and before each release.
5. If the user's immediate safety is unclear, do not use a score or infer safety from silence; offer direct human help.
6. Do not claim that a service is open, has capacity, accepted a referral or will respond within a particular time unless verified from a current authoritative source.
7. Do not present this note as an approved clinical protocol for suicide/self-harm assessment. It is a public signposting reference only.

This note summarises NHS public information in a limited way. It does not replace the full source pages, local NHS service information, NICE guidance, clinical assessment or emergency advice. Eligibility and local access routes may change.
